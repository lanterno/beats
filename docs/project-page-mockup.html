The look

Half past three on a hill above the fields. The page lives inside a painted afternoon: a watercolour sky with slow clouds, sunlight from the top right, and green hills at the foot of the screen. The figures sit on cloud-white panels that float on the painting. There are no boxes, no borders, no textures: separation is done with air, soft washes and round shapes. At dusk the same hill under an indigo sky with a band of sunset at the horizon, and one warm lamp on the running timer.

  • Colour. Sky blue and meadow green are the painting; the panels are warm white; the ink is a deep slate. One accent, sunlight yellow, marks today, the running timer and the thing you can press. Leaf green for hours over, persimmon for hours owed. Tints are soft pills: sky for vacation, blossom for sick, leaf for a public holiday.
  • Type. Two rounded faces drawn in Japan. M PLUS Rounded 1c, plump at its heaviest weight, sets the balance, the week's figures and every column of digits, which all share one width. Zen Maru Gothic sets everything read. Nothing is hand-written; the warmth is in the round letterforms.
  • Shape. Every surface is a rounded cloud, every control a pill, every highlight a wash. Rows in the day book and the ledger are separated by air and a barely-there rule, not by lines. The contract's rate changes are green pills laid across the ledger where they happened.
  • Light. The standing is the brightest cloud, with a breath of sunlight in its top right corner. Everything else is a shade quieter so the balance is the first thing read.
  • Motion. The clouds drift across two minutes. The lamp breathes. Nothing else moves, and nothing moves under reduced-motion.

What this page is now

One question per kind of project, answered in the first screen. For a day job: where do I stand with my employer as of today, and what does that make of the rest of this week? For a side project or freelance work: am I on the pace I set myself, and where did the hours go? Everything under the first screen is the evidence for that answer, in the order a person would check it.

  • The standing is the only lifted object on the page. The balance sits in the largest type with its three-term proof, and the line beneath says what Sunday looks like under the two choices a Thursday offers. Beside it, the week as three figures and one sentence: whether tomorrow is a long day, a short day, or off, and when the week resumes.
  • The open week shows its seven days once. Each weekday carries what the contract expected of it, what was worked, and its sessions underneath; today is open, the other days are one line and open on click. Holidays and absences sit on the day they cost. Booking Friday off is a click on Friday.
  • Earlier weeks is the ledger: one line per week with the adjusted expectation, the hours, the difference and the running balance, and a rule row on the date every term changed. Reading down the column is how 60% → 80% → 100% becomes visible, not listed. Runs of weeks with nothing worked and nothing expected collapse to one line.
  • The contract register in the rail draws the terms as a step function over the contract's life, names the term in force and the next one, and keeps the full list with edit, remove and "Change contract from…". On a side project the same slot holds the goal and its history, because a dated goal change is the same thing.
  • Time off is an upcoming list and a year tally. The month grid survives as the date picker inside the booking dialog, so the page has exactly one time navigator: the open week.
  • The header identifies and never measures. Dot, name, a derived kind chip, description, repo, settings. The lifetime total, the goal ring and the worked/expected pair leave it, so the header can never disagree with the figures below.

What the API already has, and what it does not

  • From the wire today: the balance, this week's expected / worked / remaining and its seven days with holiday and absence, the earlier weeks with their adjusted expectation, the terms, the absences, the holidays, the sessions. The step chart, the rule rows, the Thursday sentence, the Sunday projection, the tally and the upcoming list are all client arithmetic over those.
  • Marked needs api in the mockup: the running balance per past week and the three terms of the proof (opening, worked, expected-through). One tz-aware ledger route returning each week's worked, expected and closing balance would deliver both and fix a drift the current page inherits: the week rows are bucketed by UTC date and completed beats only, while the week card is bucketed by the request timezone and includes the running timer. Until it exists the balance column stays hidden; nothing is reconstructed client-side.

States not drawn here

  • No contract yet (day job): the balance slot reads "No contract yet. Add the terms and the page keeps a balance from that date." with Add contract; the week runs against the personal goal if any; time off is bookable (the API allows it) and counts once a contract exists.
  • Objective or 0-hour term: no balance ("no balance while it lasts"), no expectation, the personal goal if set; the register shows the term as a labelled gap or a step at zero.
  • Before the first term: "Contract starts Mon Sep 14 · 33.6 h/week", worked only. After ended_on: "Final balance +N h · ended Aug 31" with the proof frozen at that date.
  • Missing region: the register turns amber ("Public holidays are not deducted · Set region") and the proof line carries the same caveat.
  • Past week open: the balance stays pinned to today; the right half of the standing becomes "Week 35 · closed +1.5 h over", the sentence goes away, the day rows show that week.

Decisions I need from you

  • Words. The mockup keeps the app's vocabulary: Expected / Worked / Remaining / Balance. The ledger concept preferred payroll words (Due / Put in). One set app-wide; say if you want the other.
  • The first week. A fresh day job shows a large negative balance from day two, because each day is charged as it closes. The "New day job" state renders it in muted rather than red with a sentence explaining it. Keep that softer treatment for the first governed week, or show it red like any other?
  • The projection. "By Sunday: +5.7 h if the week is met · +3.8 h if you stop now" is the most quotable line on the page and the one that swings with the running timer. Keep it, or keep only the proof?
  • The plan column and the health sparkline are demoted as noted above. Both are shipped features; confirm.
  • Themes. The app has four dark themes today. This direction has two hours, afternoon and dusk, the same hill painted twice. Keep two, or make the four names variants of these two?
