# Privacy

This is a personal, open-source project. I (Ahmed Elghareeb, [@lanterno](https://github.com/lanterno)) am the only operator. This page describes — in plain English — what Beats does with your data. Beats is the project's name in code; you meet it as **LifePete** — lifepete.com, the LifePete apps for Mac, Android and iPhone, and the daemon and editor extension that feed them. This policy covers all of them.

For the catalogue of every field stored, see [DATA.md](./DATA.md).

## What Beats is

Beats is a self-tracking system. It records what you work on, for how long, and (optionally) how you felt while doing it. It is not a productivity app sold to employers, and there is no analytics product built on top of your data.

If you mark a project as a day job, Beats also stores its contract — the hours owed each week as a percentage of a full-time basis, the dates those terms changed, an opening balance — the employer's holiday region as two ISO codes, the employer's own days off where they differ from that region's (dates, with the names you give them, and the region's holidays the employer does not give), the vacation days the job gives if you track them (days per year, how unused days carry over, and the days left on a date you choose), and the days you were absent (vacation, sick or other, whole or half), and any dated adjustments you make to the balance (a payout, a reset or a cap, with a note if you give one). From those it works out what the week expects of you, a running overtime balance and the vacation days you have left.

If you bill freelance work, Beats also stores your clients — each one's name, a contact's name and email address if you give them, the currency you bill in and its dated hourly rates — which of your freelance projects is billed to which client, from when, whether its hours are billable and at what rate, the sessions you marked billable or not, and a budget you set on a client or a billed project, in hours or money. From those it works out what each month comes to per client, and how much of each budget is spent, what is left and when it may run out; none of that is stored, and no alert about it is sent anywhere. A contact's name and email address are someone else's details: Beats shows them to you, mails nothing to them and sends them nowhere. If you share a report with a client, Beats also stores that link — which client it is for, the range it covers, whether it shows amounts and entries, and its secret token — and nothing about who opens it: no visit count, no time of reading, no address.

## Who can see your data

- **You.** Through the web app, the daemon, the editor extension, the companion app, and the export endpoint.
- **Me, the operator.** I can read any document in the database. I do not log into your account or read your sessions without a reason, but I am not going to pretend a single-operator service has a privileged-access boundary it does not have. If you would not be comfortable with me being able to see something, do not put it in Beats.
- **The hosting providers** that run the infrastructure (Google Cloud for the API, MongoDB Atlas / GCP for the database, Firebase Hosting for the web app). They see encrypted-at-transit traffic and storage they manage on their side. Standard cloud-provider terms apply.
- **Your clients, only through a link you made.** A client is a record in your account, not an account of theirs, and sees nothing until you decide otherwise. You can create a **report link**: a URL for one client and a date range you fix, which anyone holding it opens without an account. It shows that client's projects by name, the hours on them in that range, and — only if you ticked the box when you made it — the rates and amounts; you can also leave out the entry-by-entry list and share the totals alone. It shows nothing else: not your other clients or what they pay, not your personal, day-job or side projects, not a project of theirs you have not billed, not your notes or tags, not your flow scores, biometrics, calendar or coach, and not whether a timer is running now. The range never moves, so the link shows the same figures every time it is opened. You can see every link a client has, and what each one shows, on the Clients page, and revoking one kills the URL immediately. Deleting a client revokes its links too. Anyone you send a link to can pass it on, so treat it as you would an emailed invoice. No paired device — a phone, the daemon, the wall clock — can read your clients, rates, billing, budgets or amounts: those routes refuse a device's token, and so does the report link's.
- **An organisation you join, only its own projects' hours.** An organisation — an employer, a team — is a party that owns projects. If you accept its invitation you keep your one account and your one timeline, its projects appear on your list marked with its name, and tracking on them works as on any other. What the organisation sees is the hours on *its* projects: each entry's start, end, duration and note; your absences on them as date, type and whole or half day; and, where you keep a contract on one, its running balance. It sees nothing else — not your personal projects or that they exist, not your tags, flow scores, apps, repositories, idle time, biometrics, calendar, coach or insights, and not whether a timer is running now; its read takes finished entries only. If you connect a project you already tracked, it sees that project's hours only from a day you choose; a contract's balance, which sums every hour since the contract began, is then withheld rather than give away what came before. An invitation reaches you as a link from the person who invited you; LifePete sends no email and, since it cannot verify an email address, never hands the way in to whoever holds one. You can see exactly what it holds about you, through the same read it uses, on the organisation's "What Acme can see" page. If the organisation manages your contract, its admins and your team's managers write the contract's terms and decide your days off and timesheets: a day off you ask for carries a note addressed to the decider — the record never shows it — and an approved month's figures are frozen and its days locked until someone reopens it with a recorded reason. Time an admin enters for you appears on your own timeline marked as entered for you, and every such act is in the organisation's audit log. A **viewer** role (an accountant, a works council seat) can read the record; nobody below admin reads the audit log; a manager sees only their teams. Leaving stops tracking to its projects and takes them off your list; the organisation keeps the hours already on them as its working-time record, and you keep a read-only copy of your own that you can download. No paired device can reach an organisation's routes. Nothing about an organisation is sent to the coach beyond the names of its projects, as any project's name is.
- **No one else.** Beats does not sell, share, or syndicate your data. There are no advertising partners. There is no analytics provider with PII.

## Authentication

Beats uses [WebAuthn passkeys](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-2/). The server never sees a password — it only stores a public key and a counter. Sessions are signed JWTs.

A deployment can also offer **single sign-on** through an OpenID Connect provider (Google, Microsoft, Keycloak, …) as a second door beside the passkey. Beats stores the identity the provider asserts — its issuer and subject, at most one per provider per account — and still never a password. Either door issues an ordinary Beats session: **nothing downstream records which door was used**, and nothing in your account behaves differently for it. You can link and unlink a provider identity in Settings; an unlink that would leave the account with no way to sign in is refused.

## Third-party integrations (all optional)

If you connect any of these, the relevant OAuth token is stored in the database and used only to fetch the data that integration is for:

- **GitHub** — read commit and repo activity to enrich beats
- **Google Calendar** — read events to align beats with meetings
- **Fitbit, Oura** — read biometric data (sleep, HRV, steps)

The phone apps do not read Apple Health or Health Connect today. If they ever do, it will be opt-in, and this page will say so before the release that adds it.

You can disconnect any integration at any time. Disconnecting revokes the token on Beats' side; revoke at the provider too if you want belt-and-braces.

## The phone apps

The LifePete app on Android and iPhone is a window onto your account; it keeps almost nothing of its own.

- **What it stores on the phone**: the device token from pairing (in Android's Keystore or the iOS Keychain), the server's address, your recent projects and a few settings (light or dusk, which notifications you want). Unpairing deletes the token.
- **What it sends**: requests to your LifePete server — the timer, sessions you log, brief ratings, coach chat — the same as the web app. Pairing sends the phone's name so you can tell devices apart in Settings.
- **Camera**: used only to scan the pairing code, on the phone. No image is stored or sent anywhere.
- **Notifications**: the morning brief, nudges and suggestions, built from your own data while the app is open. No push service is involved.
- **Google libraries**: the code scanner is Google's ML Kit, which runs on the phone and sends Google diagnostics about the scanner itself (device model, OS version, how it performed, under a random ID for this installation that names neither you nor the phone) — never the camera image or anything from your account. The app's two typefaces are fetched from Google Fonts the first time it opens, which shows Google the phone's IP address, as any web page using those fonts does.
- **Sample data**: "Look around with sample data" is made up on the phone and sends nothing.
- **No analytics, no ads, no crash reporting** services are built in.

## The coach

The coach feature sends prompts to the [Anthropic Claude API](https://www.anthropic.com/) to generate briefs, weekly reviews, and chat replies. What the prompt carries is assembled in `api/src/beats/coach/context.py`, and it is more than the timer data:

- your sessions — project name, start time, duration, and the session note if there is one
- your projects' weekly goals — on a day job, that is the contract's weekly hours, to the hundredth as the project page shows them, and where they come from (the percentage of a part-time term as the project page shows it, `full-time`, or just `contract` for custom hours), in one line (`Acme: 33.6h/week (contract, 80%)`, `Acme: 35h/week (contract, 87.5%)`)
- on a day job with a contract, this week against it, labelled as the goal line is (`Acme (contract, 80%)`, the term in force on the week's Monday, or on the contract's first day when it starts mid-week (that week the goal line does not yet carry the contract)): the hours it expects after holidays and days off, the hours worked and what is left, today's expected hours, the running overtime balance and where it would stand by Sunday, the dates of this week's holidays and days off that made the week shorter (`Fri 2026-10-16: day off`), and the contract's end date if it ends this week (`contract ends 2026-10-16`). Each figure is the one the project page shows, to one decimal as the page rounds it. The hours worked are the whole week's, as on the page; on a week the contract starts or ends inside, what is left counts only the contract's days, and the line says `(the contract counts only its own days this week)`, which, with no end date beside it, says the contract started this week. In the contract's first seven days the by-Sunday figures are not sent, as the page does not show them; the coach is told it is the first week under the contract instead, but for the contract's last day, when the page and the coach both give them. The running balance is the sum of everything the contract has counted since it started — your opening balance, the hours worked under it against the hours it expected, and your balance adjustments — and the by-Sunday figures are built on it. None of those parts is sent as a figure of its own, but the sum gives them away when the others are zero: on a contract's first day the balance is the opening balance, and on the day of a payout it moves by the payout. The adjustments themselves (their dates, kinds and notes) are not sent. A public holiday is sent by its name (`public holiday (Pentecost Monday)`). The holiday region is not sent as a field of its own, but a regional holiday's name can name the region itself — for example `Augsburg Peace Festival` or `Yap Day` — and so say where your employer is; for some regions the region's code is the place name, so the holiday's name contains it. No country code is sent. A company holiday is sent only as the words `company holiday` and its date, never the name you gave it. A day off is sent as `day off` or `half day off`: its type (vacation, sick, other) and its note are not sent. This section sends no holidays or days off outside the current week, and not your vacation entitlement, though an earlier brief's opening line, below, can repeat what that brief said of its own week. On the contract's last day it is still live, as it is on the project page, since that day's hours still count: the coach still sends today's expected hours, the running balance and the by-Sunday figures, which that day are where the contract closes. A contract that ended before today, earlier in the current week, sends only its final balance and its end date (`final balance: +4.5 h over (contract ended 2026-10-14)`); one that ended before the current week sends none of this, though its weekly-goal line above is still sent until you archive the project.
- your days and weeks are your own throughout: this section's week and today, the day of calendar events and the times they are shown at, the weekly-goal line, the weekly totals and the productivity score, the last seven days of sessions a memory rewrite reads, and what the coach looks up in a chat (your projects' goals, your sessions by date and time, your patterns) are all in your timezone. The web app sends its timezone with a brief, a chat message and a memory rewrite, and the companion with a brief, its brief notification and a chat message; a client that sends none is answered for UTC's days and weeks.
- 30-day aggregates, plus anything stored in coach memory
- how your recent briefs landed, if you rate them: your last 14 rated briefs, from any date, each by its date, your rating (`helpful` or `not useful`) and the brief's opening sentence, cut at 90 characters (`2026-10-09: helpful — opened with "Yesterday was a day off for Acme…"`). That sentence is the coach's own from that morning, so it can repeat what it said then: another week's day off or holiday, a contract's expected hours or balance as they stood that day. Clearing a rating stops its brief being sent.
- **calendar event titles and times for the day**, if Google Calendar is connected
- last night's biometrics — sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, readiness — if a health source is connected
- flow signals from the daemon, including the repository and language you spent most time in

Never sent: your clients and their contacts, rates, which projects or entries are billable, your billing budgets and what they have spent, or any amount of money. A project's name is sent as before, and may be a client's name if you named it so.

Anthropic processes that data under their terms; Beats does not send it anywhere else.

If you do not want any data leaving the Beats database, do not use the coach.

## Export and deletion

- **Export**: anytime, via `/api/export` — your projects (contract included, archived ones too, and an organisation's projects as you see them), sessions, absences and balance adjustments, and your clients (archived ones and their contacts too), each project's billing, the sessions you marked billable or not and your billing budgets, as a JSON backup you can import again (report links are deliberately left out: a backup file would then carry live URLs to your hours), or your sessions as CSV. Plans, webhooks, signals, biometrics and coach history are not in it yet.
- **Timesheet**: a day job's monthly timesheet, via `/api/projects/{id}/timesheet.csv`, is a CSV for your employer. It holds per day the hours worked and expected, any balance adjustment, the public holiday's name (a company holiday reads "company holiday"), the absence type and whether it was a half day (never the absence's note), and the balance.
- **Deletion**: email me at <ahmed.elghareeb@proton.me> from the address on your account and I will delete every document associated with your user — projects, sessions, contracts, absences, balance adjustments, clients and their contacts, project billing, billable marks, billing budgets, report links, coach history, biometrics, flow signals and paired devices — and reply to confirm. **One exception**, for an organisation you were in: the entries and absences you tracked on *its* projects are its working-time record, which an employer may be bound by law to keep, and they stay — each entry's start, end, project and note, each absence's date, type and half day, the balance adjustments and contract terms behind a balance it read — under an id that is no account's, together with the name and email address it knew you by. Your tags on those entries, your notes on those absences, your goal and your local repository paths go with the rest. Nothing on your personal projects survives. That exception is itself bounded: an organisation keeps its record only for its retention period — a setting with a statutory floor, four years by default — after which a purge the operator runs removes what it kept of a deleted account, and returns rows still on a living account to being purely their own. Uninstalling an app or unpairing a device removes what it kept on that device but does not delete your account.
